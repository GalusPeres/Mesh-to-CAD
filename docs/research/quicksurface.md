## 0. Key findings

1. **What makes QuickSurface (QS) easy:**
   - **Snap mode is on by default.** Net points stay on the scan.
   - **Deviation colours are live** in almost every tool.
   - **One gesture builds most of the net:** drag the edge handle, or Alt / Ctrl+Alt-drag. Add S (split), Q (smooth) and Space (show the result).

   Sources: yt:NgiR5Q-PDWI, yt:7RW172PjFJg, yt:mvTkw3Aly_s 3:01. Reviewers name the live deviation as the advantage over Fusion (yt:E3ah4ymgblE 11:53).

2. **The core object is an editable quad SubD.** It is G2 everywhere and G1 at extraordinary points (yt:7RW172PjFJg 9:46, yt:f2Ld8ulfPWU 4:52). On export it becomes a few **packed NURBS patches**, whose borders are the white lines (yt:NgiR5Q-PDWI 11:48). Auto Free Form, Wrap, quad-OBJ import and manual New Free Form all produce this same object.
3. **Auto Free Form is the friend's key feature and QS's weakest spot.**
   - QS's own engineers advise against it for clean parts: it ripples because it snaps to scan noise ("ice cube" zebra stripes), uses uniform quad size and gives too many patches (yt:ARnycA4A_P0 40:13, yt:atlXnNk6GVk 9:49, yt:rXOpEFYz7us 5:50, yt:C_PRHlm_iR0 54:14).
   - Users say it fails on prismatic or sharp-edged parts (develop3d 2019 review; r/1s54oo3).

   A smooth, adaptive, feature-aware auto net is our biggest lever [I].

4. **Price and paywall are the #1 complaint.**
   - Lite costs €480/yr and lacks Boolean, fillet, loft, fill, thicken, auto-trim, segmentation and feature-aligned Auto Free Form.
   - Pro costs €1,700/yr or €5,250 perpetual.
   - Personal (€20/mo) exports **STL only** (QS/price/, Matrix 2026, r/1wrr46c).
5. **The competitive clock is running.**
   - QS 2026 moved to the Siemens Parasolid kernel, which fixed users' crashes in trim and fillet (QS/whats-new-qs2026/, r/1q7fddr).
   - "QUICKSURFACE AI — mesh to editable CAD with one click" was announced on 2026-09-24 for Formnext 2026, with no details yet (QS/quicksurface-ai/).
6. **Licence traps.**
   - Open-source openNURBS does **not** contain Rhino's SubD→NURBS packing (`ON_SubD::BrepForm()` returns nullptr), so we must write it.
   - These are GPL and unusable: QuadWild, CGAL, SolveSpace, PyMeshLab, pymeshfix.
   - Our current stack is clean: PyNanoInstantMeshes (BSD), OCP/OCCT (LGPL) and three-mesh-bvh (MIT). Add planegcs (LGPL).

## C. UX principles, pain points, and how we can be easier

**What makes QS easy (verified)**

1. **Feedback is always on.** Live tolerance colours in freeform, fit surface, sketches and curves; 2026 adds "live analysis in all key features" (yt:7RW172PjFJg 11:29, yt:yWB7zbiqSAQ 1:10, QS/whats-new-qs2026/).
2. **Snap by default, and the brush keeps snapping.** Fusion users must re-run Pull after every smooth (yt:mvTkw3Aly_s 10:55).
3. **A few gestures:** hover handle, Alt-drag, double-click chain, S, Space, Shift+paint fit. All nets are one object type (yt:ARnycA4A_P0 48:02).
4. **Problems are visible and clickable:** red irregular points, white patch borders, orange creases, coloured trim pieces with a "solid" status.
5. **One-click presets with sane defaults:** Balanced auto, Fit "Auto" at 95%, auto-constrain at 0.5°, auto-fillet radius from the scan, the Prepare Scan wizard.
6. **Robust hybrid, non-destructive:** offset-by-reference, then auto-trim; parametric primitives and trims; "keep original mesh"; a history marker (yt:f2Ld8ulfPWU 19:27, yt:AVgONbXU0eg).

**Pain points**

- **Price and paywall.** This is the top complaint. Lite and Revo Design can't finish a part: no Boolean, no fillet (forum.revopoint3d.com/t/revodesign-problem/35704). Personal is STL-only, "a mesh cleanup tool" at best (r/1wrr46c). Occasional users want pay-as-you-go (r/1rv0jf1). Lite workflows end in Fusion, stitching 366 bodies (yt:C_PRHlm_iR0 29:02).
- **Auto Free Form quality.** In QS's own demo of manual vs auto, about 2,000 quads ripple (yt:ARnycA4A_P0 40:13). Quad sizes are uniform (yt:rXOpEFYz7us 5:50). It produces too many patches for the customer (yt:C_PRHlm_iR0 54:14). It fails at sharp edges and prismatic parts (develop3d.com 2019 review; r/1s54oo3). Users fall back to "square faces all over the part one by one" (r/1s54oo3). Symmetry worsens the fit (yt:C_PRHlm_iR0 35:31).
- **Preparation burden.** Ragged rims, holes and logos must be fixed first (yt:ARnycA4A_P0 46:44, yt:E3ah4ymgblE 15:40). A whole mesh is slow, so users isolate regions (yt:03mfpMIwxcU 1:58). There is a TEMP-folder failure (yt:NfeYTpfWdZc). Users call the hole filling poor (r/1lrptzu).
- **Edges and trimming.** The hardest part is rebuilding edges cleanly. Trim removes the wrong regions, and a midplane came out 3 mm off (r/1s81nlg). Trims drop faces and Fill crashes (pre-2026; r/1dt4zhp). Sew fails after fillet (r/1dt4zhp). Fillets hit kernel limits (yt:atlXnNk6GVk 28:00-35:43). Thicken self-intersects, with an 8-step workaround (yt:6r-PE8TBEyQ).
- **Hidden expertise.** Chords such as Ctrl+Alt and Ctrl+Shift, and a brush that is not in the context menu (yt:NgiR5Q-PDWI 9:11). Closest-point snapping onto hidden geometry led to the ray mode (yt:sKpW7eZOdaQ). Tutorials are outdated or too fast (r/1dt4zhp, r/1ocnunw). Alignment is confusing, e.g. centring on the origin (reddit.com/r/quicksurface/comments/1uu55im).
- **Platform.** Windows only, with online activation. AMD viewport slowness (r/1t8m744). Users fear the new AI feature will sit behind top tiers (r/1wteedm).

**How we can be easier than QS [I]**

1. **"Prepare scan" as one guarded button.** It decimates while showing % within tolerance, denoises, fills holes, de-jags rims and removes small pieces. It finds the **symmetry plane automatically** and offers 3-2-1 alignment with "centre on origin".
2. **An auto net that is smooth by construction.**
   - Fit the control points by regularised least squares to all scan points instead of snapping vertices (no ripples).
   - Make density curvature-adaptive.
   - Detect hard edges automatically and turn them into crease chains.
   - Fix rim irregular points automatically.
   - Give one "smooth ↔ accurate" slider with live % within tolerance, and pack the export into the fewest patches.
3. **Prismatic-aware auto.** Detect planes and cylinders first and freeform only the rest. Offset-by-reference and auto-trim run implicitly, so the user gets one "Make solid" button.
4. **Visible gestures.** Every chord is also a button. A status bar shows what Alt, Ctrl or Shift will do now. Hover previews the duplicated face. Default snap = ray along the view or normal, front faces only.
5. **A button wizard (remote use case).** Click a button on the scan. The app slices at mid-height, auto-fits a circle, slot or rounded rectangle with inferred constraints, extrudes "up to scan", then cuts and fillets with a measured radius. That is one dialog instead of about six QS steps.
6. **Explainable failures.** When trim, sew or thicken fails, highlight the gap or self-intersection and offer a fix. Never silently drop faces.
7. **Free, cross-platform, no activation, with in-app hints.** Real STEP, trim, Boolean, fillet and feature-aligned auto — what Lite and Personal withhold. Each tool gets a 1-line hint, versioned with the app.

## D. Algorithms for the P1 tools

Licences were checked on the repo LICENSE files or PyPI by the research agent. The choices themselves are [I].

**Avoid (GPL, AGPL or not open):**

- CGAL (Shape_detection, VSA, PMP hole filling)
- SolveSpace, python-solvespace, py-slvs
- QuadWild, quadwild-bimdf, libQEx, pyquadwild (labelled MIT, wraps GPL)
- PyMeshLab, pymeshfix (GPL-3, or AGPL on PyPI), MeshFix
- Splipy, libigl `copyleft/`
- Schnabel's original RANSAC code (research-only)

1. **Prepare the scan.**
   - Reduce with QEM: `fast-simplification` (MIT, already used).
   - Denoise with Taubin λ|μ (doi:10.1145/218380.218473), `trimesh.smoothing.filter_taubin` (MIT).
   - Fill holes with Liepa 2003 (doi:10.2312/SGP/SGP03/200-206): a DP minimum-weight triangulation, then refinement, then bilaplacian fairing. Port `pmp-library` `fill_hole` (MIT), or use libigl `harmonic(k=2)` (MPL-2). For fitting, give holes zero weight instead.
2. **Magic wand and brush (JS).**
   - Magic wand: BFS over typed-array face adjacency; stop when the dihedral angle exceeds θ or the angle to the seed normal exceeds θ_global. Run it on a decimated proxy above 1M triangles.
   - Visible brush: GPU ID buffer.
   - Select-through and lasso: three-mesh-bvh shapecast and `selection` example (MIT).
3. **Symmetry plane.**
   1. Candidates: the PCA planes, plus planes voted from point pairs (Mitra, Guibas, Pauly 2006, doi:10.1145/1141911.1141924).
   2. Refine with reflective ICP using robust weights (Combès 2008, doi:10.1109/cvpr.2008.4587605). The normal is the dominant eigenvector of Σ(p−q)(p−q)ᵀ.
   3. Score by RMS and inlier %, and show the mirrored deviation map.
4. **Alignment.**
   - Store it as a non-destructive 4×4 feature.
   - 3-2-1 from features.
   - Kabsch/Umeyama for point pairs.
   - Point-to-plane ICP (Chen & Medioni 1992; Open3D MIT or trimesh) for fine alignment.
5. **Primitive fitting.**
   1. Algebraic start: SVD plane, Pratt sphere, cylinder axis from the normal covariance.
   2. Geometric refinement with `scipy.optimize.least_squares` (Lukács, Martin, Marshall 1998, doi:10.1007/BFb0055697). Port the GeometricTools formulas (BSL-1.0).
   3. Snap to parallel or perpendicular when within 0.5–1.5°, then re-solve.
   4. Auto-detection: our own normal-aware RANSAC (Schnabel 2007, doi:10.1111/j.1467-8659.2007.01016.x).
6. **Section sketch auto-fit (buttons).**
   1. `trimesh` section → polylines → RDP breakpoints.
   2. Split-and-merge into lines and arcs (Rosin & West 1995, doi:10.1109/34.476507). Fit arcs with Hyper/Taubin (`circle-fit`, MIT), then refine geometrically.
   3. Recognise templates (circle, slot, rounded rectangle).
   4. Infer constraints by thresholds (Langbein et al. 2004, doi:10.1016/S0010-4485(03)00108-8).
   5. Solve with **planegcs** (FreeCAD solver, LGPL-2.1): `pip planegcs` and npm `@salusoft89/planegcs` (WASM) for live dragging.
7. **Auto Free Form.**
   1. Decimate to 20–50k triangles.
   2. Remesh with **PyNanoInstantMeshes** (BSD; Jakob et al. 2015, doi:10.1145/2816795.2818078): `vertex_count≈target/4`, `creaseAngle` 30–45°, `align_to_boundaries=True`.
   3. One Catmull-Clark split gives pure quads.
   4. Symmetry: slice the scan, remesh one half, mirror, weld the seam.
   5. **Fit, don't snap:** least squares of scan-point→limit-surface distance with a net-Laplacian regulariser, then re-project and iterate (Hoppe et al. 1994, doi:10.1145/192161.192233; Marinov & Kobbelt 2005, doi:10.1016/j.gmod.2005.01.003; MIT reference code awf/OpenSubdiv-Model-Fitting).
   6. Upgrades: a QuadriFlow pybind (BSD); QuadWild only as an optional external GPL executable. Background: Bommes et al. 2013 survey (doi:10.1111/cgf.12014) and Eck & Hoppe 1996 (doi:10.1145/237170.237271).
8. **SubD and packed NURBS export.**
   - Catmull-Clark in numpy with DeRose 1998 creases (doi:10.1145/280814.280826).
   - Regular quads become exact bicubic patches. Merge rectangular regular blocks into one `Geom_BSplineSurface`.
   - Around extraordinary points, subdivide 1–2 levels, then use ACC (Loop & Schaefer 2008, doi:10.1145/1330511.1330519) or a G1 `BRepFill_Filling` n-gon.
   - Sew the result.
   - Reference implementation: OpenSubdiv `ENDCAP_BSPLINE_BASIS` (Apache-variant licence).
9. **Snap and brush.**
   - Snap the **limit point**: limit = (n²v + 4Σe + Σf)/(n(n+5)), so move the control point by Δ·(n+5)/n.
   - Ray-cast on pick, closest point on drag, front faces only.
   - Brush: Taubin on the net Laplacian with Wendland falloff, pinned rim and crease vertices, then re-projection.
10. **Fit surface.** Scattered least-squares B-spline with fixed knots: `scipy.sparse.linalg.lsqr` plus a thin-plate term (Weiss et al. 2002, doi:10.1016/S0167-8396(01)00086-3), with parameter correction. "Auto" grows U×V until 95% of points are within tolerance.
11. **Offset-by-reference, trim, Boolean** (OCP Apache-2.0 / OCCT LGPL).
    - Offset-by-reference: push net vertices whose limit points lie within tolerance of a primitive outward until the signed distance exceeds the tolerance.
    - Trim: `BOPAlgo_Splitter`, classify cells by scan coverage (BVH distance on sample points), sew, then `ShapeFix_Solid`.
    - Before thickening, check that the minimum radius of curvature exceeds the thickness.
12. **Deviation and zebra.**
    - Deviation: three-mesh-bvh `closestPointToPoint` in a worker, against a fine `BRepMesh` tessellation. Diverging ramp symmetric about 0 (Moreland 2009), green within ±tolerance, plus a histogram and % within tolerance.
    - Zebra: shader `step(.5, fract(k·dot(reflect(-V,N), axis)))` on analytic normals (Klass 1980).
